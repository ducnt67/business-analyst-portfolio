# Harvard-style CV rules and QA checklist

This reference consolidates the user's source material into reusable rules. Use it when drafting, reviewing, or tailoring a CV.

## 1. Fit and boundaries

- Best suited to banking, consulting, finance, law, academia, research/R&D, analytics, accounting, engineering, IT, and other roles where clarity and evidence matter.
- It is not automatically the best choice for design-led or highly creative roles such as UX/UI or some digital-marketing positions. Use a restrained version only if it still represents the candidate well and meets the submission requirements.
- Confirm any employer or university-specific format requirement before applying generic rules.

## 2. Layout and file rules

- Aim for one page when the candidate has under roughly seven years of experience and can present the evidence clearly. Use two pages only when seniority, academic work, research, or specialized experience genuinely requires it.
- Use a readable professional font such as Times New Roman, Arial, Calibri, or Garamond. A practical default is 10–11 pt body text and 14–16 pt section or name hierarchy, adjusted to avoid crowding.
- Prefer black and white. At most one restrained accent color, such as navy or dark gray, may be used if it does not reduce contrast or parsing reliability.
- Use consistent spacing, approximately 1.0–1.15 line spacing and 6–10 pt separation between sections as a starting point. Adjust for legibility and page fit.
- Avoid photos, decorative graphics, text boxes, complex tables, columns that scramble reading order, icons used as the only labels, headers/footers containing essential contact details, and visual rating bars.
- Submit PDF unless the recipient specifies another format. Use a clear filename such as `FullName_CV_TargetRole.pdf`.

## 3. Header and contact information

Include:

- Full name.
- Professional email.
- Phone number, including country code when relevant.
- LinkedIn and/or portfolio link when relevant and functional.
- City or region rather than a full street address unless the application explicitly requires it.

Keep the header easy to scan in a few seconds. Do not include unnecessary personal data, informal email addresses, or dead links.

## 4. Education

Include the institution and city, degree and major, graduation year, and relevant academic distinctions. Include GPA only when it is strong and provide the scale, for example `3.5/4.0` or `8.2/10`. For students and recent graduates, place Education before Experience when it strengthens the application; otherwise place it after the most relevant experience.

## 5. Experience and achievements

For each role, show organization, title, location when useful, and dates with a consistent format. Use present tense for a current role and past tense for completed roles.

Prioritize outcomes over responsibilities. Quantify with percentages, money, volume, people, time, accuracy, ranking, or other defensible measures. Use action verbs such as Led, Designed, Increased, Optimized, Implemented, Analyzed, Automated, Reduced, or Delivered, but choose verbs that accurately reflect the candidate's contribution.

Use the XYZ pattern:

`Accomplished X, as measured by Y, by doing Z.`

Example pattern (adapt to verified facts only): `Increased email conversion to 35% over six months by segmenting audiences and personalizing campaigns, contributing to VND 2.4B in revenue.`

## 6. Skills and additional sections

- Group skills into relevant categories such as Technical, Tools, Languages, or Domain Knowledge.
- Include proficiency levels, scores, or certifications only when they are accurate and useful.
- Prefer demonstrable technical skills over generic soft-skill lists such as “communication”, “teamwork”, or “time management”.
- Include 2–4 strong projects when they provide relevant evidence, especially for students, career changers, and early-career candidates. Describe contribution and result with the XYZ pattern.
- Include relevant extracurricular leadership, competitions, volunteering, or hackathons when they strengthen the target application.
- Include certifications only when relevant, current, and verifiable.

## 7. Common failure modes

1. **Wrong format for the context**: using a plain Harvard style where a portfolio or visually expressive format is expected.
2. **Ignoring the target role or employer**: submitting the same generic CV everywhere.
3. **Weak verbs**: leading with “Responsible for” or “Worked on” instead of showing ownership.
4. **No evidence**: describing duties without outcomes or measurable scope.
5. **Irrelevant content**: spending scarce space on unrelated skills or experience.
6. **Blind copy-paste**: leaving incorrect dates, recipient details, titles, links, or template text.
7. **No final proofread**: spelling, grammar, tense, alignment, inconsistent punctuation, or broken links.

## 8. Final QA checklist

### Fit

- [ ] Target role, employer/program, and audience are known or assumptions are stated.
- [ ] The chosen style suits the role and submission context.
- [ ] The most relevant evidence is visible in the top half of page one.

### Content

- [ ] Every claim is supported by the user's facts.
- [ ] Experience bullets emphasize outcomes, scope, and contribution.
- [ ] Most bullets contain a defensible metric or concrete scale.
- [ ] Skills and projects are relevant and demonstrable.
- [ ] Dates, job titles, company names, locations, and graduation details are consistent.

### Language

- [ ] Bullets begin with precise action verbs.
- [ ] Tense is consistent with current versus past roles.
- [ ] No filler, repetition, unexplained acronyms, vague pronouns, or inflated claims.
- [ ] Spelling, grammar, punctuation, capitalization, and bilingual terminology are checked.

### Format and delivery

- [ ] Page count is justified by experience level and content.
- [ ] Typography, margins, spacing, alignment, and date formatting are consistent.
- [ ] Essential information is selectable text and appears in the correct reading order.
- [ ] Contact links work and the PDF opens correctly after export.
- [ ] Filename is clear and role-specific.
